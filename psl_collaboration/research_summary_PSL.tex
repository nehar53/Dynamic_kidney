{\Large\bfseries Fairness over time in dynamic kidney exchange}\\[2pt]
{\large Summary of current research}\\[4pt]
{\small Dr Yousef Amer (Adelaide University, Australia) and Neha (Manipal University Jaipur, India)}

\section*{1. Background and problem}
Kidney exchange gives patients with a willing but incompatible living donor another
route to a transplant: pairs swap donors through short cycles, or through chains
started by an altruistic donor. In Australia and New Zealand this is run by the
Australian and New Zealand Paired Kidney Exchange (ANZKX), which holds 60--90
registered pairs at a time. About 30\% of its patients are highly sensitised and very
hard to match.

The pool is cleared again and again. Patients who are not matched wait for the next
round, and while they wait some withdraw, some receive a deceased-donor kidney, and new
pairs arrive. Fairness, however, is usually judged within a single round. A patient
passed over today may be matched next month or may leave the pool for good, so the real
cost of a fair decision, and who bears it, only shows over the whole sequence of rounds.
We therefore ask how the price of fairness evolves from round to round, and whether
clearing every month or waiting for a larger pool better balances the number of
transplants against fair access for hard-to-match patients.

All of our work so far uses a synthetic simulator informed by public ANZKX information;
it has not been validated on patient-level data.

\section*{3. Our optimisation framework}
Each clearing round is a four-objective problem:

\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}llc@{}}
\toprule
Objective & Meaning & Direction \\
\midrule
$F_1$ Transplant volume   & Number of patients transplanted & max \\
$F_2$ Waiting-time equity & Gini coefficient of waiting times & min \\
$F_3$ Access gap          & Match-rate gap, highly sensitised vs.\ all patients & min \\
$F_4$ Logistics gap       & Match-rate gap, distant vs.\ central hospitals (proxy) & min \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The price of fairness in a round is the share of possible transplants given up for
fairness. Following it over time shows whether fairness causes a real loss or only a
delay.

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{ILP-guided NSGA-III.} An integer linear program (ILP) first finds the
        largest matching. This matching seeds NSGA-III, is preferred when solutions are
        repaired and is kept in an archive, so the trade-off front always contains a
        matching of maximum size. Small rounds are solved exactly.
  \item \textbf{Adaptive operator selection.} A bandit, stateless (UCB1) or contextual
        (LinUCB), adjusts the mutation rate during the search.
  \item \textbf{Selection rule.} One matching is implemented from the front, subject to
        a floor on the share of the maximum volume that must be kept.
  \item \textbf{Benchmarks.} An exact two-stage ILP that finds the fairest matching of
        maximum size, a volume-maximising ILP and a lexicographic priority rule.
  \item \textbf{Evaluation.} Monthly and batched clearing; all methods tested on the
        same simulated patients with paired statistics, sensitivity analyses and public
        PrefLib instances.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Current status.} The framework and simulator are complete. Early results
suggest that fairness costs little over time when the pool is cleared monthly, that
waiting for a larger pool does not help once patients can leave, and that an exact
method is preferable at maximum volume while the evolutionary front is useful when some
volume can be traded for fairness.

\section*{4. Opportunities for collaboration}
\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{@{}p{4.2cm}L@{}}
\toprule
Partner expertise & Possible joint work \\
\midrule
Market design and kidney exchange policy
  & Clearing and batching rules that use information about departures; comparison with
    national programmes; theory for dynamic matching with fairness \\
Fair division and social choice
  & Axioms for the fairness criteria and the selection rule; fairness defined over time
    rather than per round \\
Multi-objective optimisation
  & Exact or approximate Pareto fronts below maximum volume; better selection rules;
    scaling to larger pools \\
Health operations and economics
  & Richer models of deceased-donor and transport constraints; value of waiting time;
    a validation study with programme data, subject to ethics approval \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}

We bring a dynamic simulator with operational constraints, exact and evolutionary
optimisation methods, and a tested experimental pipeline. We would welcome a
conversation about any of these questions and the chance to develop one of them into
joint work.
